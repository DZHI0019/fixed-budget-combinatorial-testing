# A six-level, three-way test plan for 10,609 factors

The included CSV contains 2,340 complete tests on 10,609 factors. Each factor can take the values 0 through 5. For every three distinct factors, every one of the 216 assignments occurs in some test. Deleting columns gives a plan of the same size for fewer factors.

## Construction and proof

Index the columns by (a,b) in the grid over the prime field of order 103, in lexicographic order. Consider the three functions a, b, and a+b, with addition in the field. Put a six-level strength-three covering array on the 103 possible values of each function. The seed is the 103-column restriction of Colbourn's known 624-row cyclotomic array. Its six constant rows can be shared between the three copies. The three blocks together therefore cost (6+3(624-6)=1860) tests. The generator recreates the seed from the powers of 5 modulo 103. [Colbourn's cyclotomy paper](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10623-009-9333-8) · [Published seed](https://github.com/chrispbsmolen/ca-tools/blob/d7394120eb9703fbd92e5a28cbbda970c298f115/table/arrays/CA_624_3_104_6.txt)

If one of the three grid functions gives three different indices, its seed block covers every requested assignment. A pair of distinct grid points can agree under at most one function because any two of the functions determine the point. Thus if all three functions have a collision, each identifies a different pair. After reordering, the three points are (a,b), (a,b+d), and (a+d,b) for a nonzero field element (d). This follows by taking the point common to the equal-(a) and equal-(b) pairs as the first point. Equality under (a+b) for the remaining pair forces their two displacements to be equal. For a requested assignment with a repeated symbol, use the function that identifies the two points assigned the same symbol. The third point has a different index and the seed realizes the prescribed values on those two indices.

For a request with three distinct symbols on this exceptional triangle, the values of (2a+b) are (x,x+d,x+2d). Define four colourings (h_c(x)=((cx) mod 103) mod 6), for (c=102,68,57,54), using least nonnegative residues. Exhaustive calculation on the 5,253 different unordered progressions confirms that at least one colouring gives three distinct colours. Act on each colouring by the 120 linear fractional transformations of the projective line over the field of order 5, identifying infinity with the symbol 5. This group is sharply three-transitive, so every assignment of three distinct output symbols occurs. These four families cost 480 tests, giving (1860+480=2340). The exported-array checker also verifies all 120 assignments on every progression directly. [Earlier use of projective transformations and perfect hash families in strength-three arrays](https://doi.org/10.1023/A:1008379710317)

## Reproduction

With Python 3.9 or later, run the following commands in this folder.

    python threeway_six.py
    python verify_csv.py test-plan.csv.gz

To produce a fresh compressed CSV, run `python threeway_six.py --csv-gz reproduced.csv.gz`. The CSV has no header. Each line represents one complete test, and column number (103a+b), beginning at zero, represents factor (a,b). The SHA-256 of the uncompressed row-major values, each stored as one byte in 0 through 5, is `1eb464f4a6eab128f8176ff052727759ff69b090666301d2fce09017c594dc3a`.

The checker reads all 24,825,060 cells, verifies that the rows are constant on the fibers specified by the four grid functions, and verifies the required ordered assignments on each of the 176,851 ordinary seed triples and every exceptional progression. It imports no generator code and requires no assumptions about the seed, projective group, or particular colourings once the CSV is given. The elementary grid argument above reduces global coverage to these finite checks.

## What the comparison establishes

At the snapshot labelled 2026-11-25, the ca-tools staircase gave 2,492 tests at 10,000 columns and 3,029 at 10,609 columns. Its additional table of recursively derived values gave 2,900 tests at 12,500 columns, hence also at 10,609. Relative to the respective better listed comparisons, the reductions are 152 (6.10%) and 560 (19.31%). A four-projection square construction applied directly to this same seed, with all six constant rows shared, already gives 2,478 at 10,609. The 138-row difference between 2,478 and 2,340 measures the improvement over that explicit comparator. [Pinned staircase](https://github.com/chrispbsmolen/ca-tools/blob/d7394120eb9703fbd92e5a28cbbda970c298f115/table/ca_frame.csv) · [Pinned derived values](https://github.com/chrispbsmolen/ca-tools/blob/d7394120eb9703fbd92e5a28cbbda970c298f115/table/derived.csv) · [Heterogeneous hash families and covering arrays](https://doi.org/10.1090/conm/523/10309)

For widths 10,000 and 10,609, the Chateauneuf–Kreher doubling formula with the pinned ingredients gives 3,086 and 3,106 tests respectively. The Cohen–Colbourn–Ling multiplication formula gives 3,213 and 3,237 when optimized over the available six-symbol difference-array entries in the ca-tools table (multipliers three and four). Both minima use multiplier four. The field-specific generalized Roux construction assumes a prime-power alphabet. Six is composite and is not a prime power. These checks do not exhaust other possible difference arrays or combinations of constructions. [Doubling and multiplication formulas, Theorems 4.2–4.4](https://doi.org/10.1002/jcd.21963) · [Pinned difference-array parameters](https://github.com/chrispbsmolen/ca-tools/blob/d7394120eb9703fbd92e5a28cbbda970c298f115/r/livingtable/data/dcan_table.csv)

We also compared the published Quantavane families, including wider designs and reductions from larger alphabets through the listed symbol-fusion rule. The lowest qualifying count in their named families is 3,029 (3,031 at seven symbols and 22,534 columns, reduced to six symbols). Table 5.6 of K. Shokri's dissertation lists no result at these widths after the corresponding column-deletion and symbol-fusion checks. We did not establish an exhaustive worldwide priority claim or an optimal lower bound. [Quantavane families](https://github.com/arhancanli/quantavane-covering-arrays/tree/0b179b520c9eb8b6c2ce8ea6273403a8d861c608) · [Wider families and fusion rule](https://github.com/arhancanli/quantavane-covering-arrays/blob/0b179b520c9eb8b6c2ce8ea6273403a8d861c608/wide/README.md) · [Shokri's dissertation](https://doi.org/10.20381/ruor-31663)

This is a construction for unconstrained factors. Applying it to a product requires its six-level factors and admissible joint configurations to match that model. The coverage theorem establishes a property of the test matrix. No product defect rate or test-runtime improvement has been measured here.
